---
title: "Graduating from Sales Onboarding"
description: "Graduation requirements, assessment criteria, and manager guidance for the GitLab Revenue Academy."
---

The GitLab Revenue Academy graduation is designed to signal that a new hire is **field-ready** for their role:

1. Able to pitch GitLab
2. Run value-based conversations
3. Connect our platform to measurable outcomes, not just complete passive learning.

Graduation is based on successful completion of all four Academy components plus a role-specific stand-and-deliver assessment, with final decisions made at **hiring manager discretion** within a consistent framework.

---

## 1. Core Graduation Requirements

New hires are expected to complete all four Academy components and meet the checkpoints below. These requirements apply to all Academy cohorts unless otherwise noted.

### Component 1: GitLab Foundations and Role-Based eLearning

New hires must:

- Complete **GitLab Foundations** plus their **role-based LevelUp/University learning path** (AE, SA, CSM, RM, xDR, etc.), including AI/Duo, DevOps, Security modernization tracks, and Revenue Org intros.
- **Pass all associated knowledge checks** in the assigned paths.
- Reach the first checkpoint by approximately the end of **Week 3** of onboarding.

Completion and scores are tracked in LevelUp/University and Academy progress reports and are shared with hiring managers as a key input into graduation decisions.

### Component 2: Value Sales Methodology (Command of the Message)

New hires must:

- Attend the full **Command of the Message (CoM) / value sales methodology class**, typically delivered as four 2-hour live virtual sessions covering GitLab's sales methodology, sales process, and value messaging.
- Where live attendance is not possible (e.g., those in the APJ region), **complete the full set of recordings and any required practice sessions** within the timelines communicated for their cohort.

Attendance and/or recording completion is mandatory and tracked as part of Academy measurement.

### Component 3: Role-Based Skills Development and Shadowing

New hires must:

- Attend all **role-based skills workshops** scheduled for their cohort and role (AE, xDR, SA, CX, etc.), or complete the full recording and associated exercises when live attendance is not possible and their manager has approved the exception.
- Complete assigned **shadowing and field practice**, using the guided shadow plan with their manager and buddy to apply Academy learning in real scenarios.

This component focuses on practical application (discovery, demos, pipeline generation, success planning, forecasting, etc.) and is a key input into the manager's view of field-readiness.

### Component 4: In-Person Graduation Summit and Stand-and-Deliver Assessment

New hires are expected to:

- Attend their assigned **in-person Academy Graduation Summit**, a 2 to 2.5 day regional event (AMER, EMEA, APJ) featuring cross-functional workshops and role-specific certification activities.
- Participate fully in the **stand-and-deliver capstone assessment**, demonstrating their ability to apply the GitLab value selling methodology in a realistic prospect or customer scenario.
- Achieve a **"Pass" rating** on the stand-and-deliver rubric for their role.

The formal checkpoint for this component is: **"Attend the in-person event and pass the role-specific capstone assessment."**

**Default rule:** Attending the regional Summit and passing the stand-and-deliver assessment is required to graduate from GitLab Revenue Academy.

## 2. How Graduation Is Assessed

### Data Sources Used in Graduation Decisions

Academy graduation decisions are supported by:

- **Course completion and knowledge checks** (Component 1)
- **Attendance and/or recording completion** for CoM and role-based skills workshops (Components 2–3)
- **Shadowing and on-the-job practice and application** between the new hire, manager, and onboarding buddy (Components 1–3)
- **Performance in the stand-and-deliver capstone assessment** during the in-person Graduation Summit (Component 4)

These inputs are consolidated into Academy progress reporting and shared with hiring managers to provide clearer readiness signals and make coaching and forecasting easier.

### Stand-and-Deliver Assessment Outcomes

The stand-and-deliver capstone is scored using a standardized rubric tailored to each role group. While the exact rubric may vary, outcomes will be recorded at a minimum as:

- **Merit** — The new hire meets all **Pass** criteria and significantly exceeds expectations on key dimensions (e.g., exceptional clarity of value narrative, advanced objection handling, and strong executive presence). Merit can be used to recognize **top performers** and is still considered a full **Pass** for graduation purposes.
- **Pass** — The new hire demonstrates field-ready mastery of the Academy learning objectives for their role (e.g., clear value narrative, effective discovery, differentiation, and linking to customer outcomes).
- **Needs Development** — The new hire has meaningful gaps in applying the methodology or role-specific skills in a prospect or customer scenario and requires additional coaching, practice, or re-assessment before graduation.

Role-based exercises may apply varying score bands to the rubric (e.g., **Merit = ≥90**, **Pass = 80–89**, **Needs Development <80**). **Merit and Pass are both treated as "passing" outcomes for graduation**, with Merit serving to highlight top performers.

## 3. Manager Discretion and Exceptions

### Manager's Role in Graduation Decisions

While Academy provides the structured program, checkpoints, rubrics, and reporting, **hiring managers remain accountable for role readiness and core onboarding**, including the new hire onboarding issue, buddy assignment, and protecting time for Academy work.

Consistent with our program design, **passing criteria for the Academy (including the stand-and-deliver panel assessment) are ultimately at hiring manager discretion, within standardized guidance and this framework.**

Managers should use Academy data as the default signal, then apply judgment based on:

- The new hire's actual performance in the field
- Timing and logistics constraints (e.g., illness, travel issues, regional conflicts)
- The broader context of ramp expectations for their role and region

### Default Expectation vs. Exceptions

**Default expectation**

By default, a new hire **graduates** when all of the following are true:

1. Component 1: Required GitLab Foundations and role-based eLearning courses are completed and knowledge checks are passed by the Week 3 checkpoint (or within an agreed extension window).
2. Component 2: CoM / methodology class is fully completed (live sessions and/or approved recordings) within the cohort's schedule.
3. Component 3: Role-based skills workshops and required shadowing are completed with active participation.
4. Component 4: The new hire **attends the in-person Graduation Summit** and **earns a "Pass" (or "Merit") rating** on the stand-and-deliver assessment.

**Manager-approved exceptions**

Managers may exercise discretion when:

- The new hire **misses a live virtual session** but completes the full recording and any associated exercises within the required timeframe, with written manager approval.
- There are **minor gaps** in LMS progress (e.g., one or two overdue micro-courses) but the manager has strong evidence of field-ready performance in the field.
- There are **meaningful gaps in required coursework** (e.g., multiple missing modules or knowledge checks) — the expectation is that they **do not attend an Academy Summit**. They should first complete a targeted **make-up plan** (see "Handling Below-Passing Performance or Non-Completion") before attendance is approved. In edge cases where the manager believes the new hire is clearly field-ready despite these gaps, attendance may be approved **only with a documented exception** and clear remediation plan.
- The new hire's **assessment score is at or just below the formal passing threshold**, but panel feedback and manager observations indicate they are performing at the expected level in actual prospect or customer conversations.

In these cases, the manager may approve graduation, paired with a **targeted coaching plan** (e.g., additional practice sessions, deal reviews, or focused shadowing).

### Handling Below-Passing Performance or Non-Completion

When a new hire **does not** meet the Academy requirements — for example:

- Significant portions of the eLearning path are incomplete (including cases where Summit attendance was approved and the stand-and-deliver result is "Pass" but required learning is still missing)
- Multiple required workshops are missed without approved make-ups
- The stand-and-deliver result is **"Needs Development"**
- There are ongoing concerns about field-readiness despite Academy participation

…the expectation is that the new hire **does not graduate yet** and instead follows a remediation and re-assessment path, owned by the hiring manager and supported by Enablement.

Recommended options include:

**1. Make-up coursework and workshops**

- Complete all missing asynchronous courses and knowledge checks.
- Attend future live skills workshops (or complete the recording and exercises) to close gaps specific to their role.

**2. Re-attending an in-person Graduation Summit**

- In some cases, the manager may determine that the new hire should **repeat the in-person Summit** to re-do the stand-and-deliver exercises and benefit from the full in-person experience before granting graduation.

**3. Virtual stand-and-deliver re-assessment**

- For cases where waiting for the next in-person Summit is not practical, the manager can schedule a **virtual stand-and-deliver assessment** using the same rubric, with a panel that includes at least the hiring manager and an Enablement Lead or Enablement Consultant.
- A **"Pass"** on this virtual re-assessment can satisfy the capstone requirement for graduation.

Decisions between these paths should factor in role expectations, timing, and regional Summit cadence (AMER/EMEA approximately every other month, APJ quarterly).

### Documentation and Communication of Exceptions

To maintain consistency and transparency, **all exceptions** (graduating with partial completion, virtual re-assessment, or requiring a re-do at a future Summit) should be documented by the hiring manager, including:

- Which requirements were not met
- Rationale for the decision (e.g., demonstrated field performance, unavoidable conflict, health issues)
- Any required remediation or follow-up plan

Managers should share exception decisions with their **regional/functional VP/AVP/Director and Enablement Lead** so we maintain consistent standards across cohorts and roles.

This approach preserves **standardized graduation gates and accountability** while honoring manager judgment on whether a new hire is truly field-ready at the end of Academy.
